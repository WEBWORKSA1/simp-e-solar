Plug-in solar, also called balcony solar, is the smallest way to make your own electricity at home. You hang one to four panels on a balcony rail, a fence or a patio, and plug them into a normal wall outlet. No roof work, no permit stack, no 20-year contract.

It will not erase your power bill. What it does well is cover the power your home uses all day long, like the fridge, the router and devices on standby. This guide explains how it works, what it really makes, how to stay safe, what it costs and where it is legal. To run numbers for your own home, try our plug-in solar estimator.

How does plug-in solar work?

Solar panels make direct current (DC). Your home runs on alternating current (AC). A small box called a microinverter sits behind the panels and turns DC into AC that matches the grid.

That AC power flows through a cord into a wall outlet. Normally power flows out of an outlet. Here it flows in, which is called backfeeding. Once it reaches your home's wiring, it goes to whatever is running nearby. Your fridge does not care where its power comes from, so it uses solar first and pulls less from the grid.

What keeps it from shocking anyone?

Two safety features matter most. First, anti-islanding: if the grid goes down, the microinverter shuts off within a fraction of a second. That protects line workers fixing the outage. It also means a plug-in kit does not give you backup power in a blackout. For that, see our guide to power outage backup.

Second, the plug itself. A certified microinverter will not send power to the plug's prongs unless it senses a live grid connection. So the bare prongs are not live when you unplug it.

How much electricity does a plug-in kit make?

Output depends on the kit size, your sunshine and how well the panels face the sun. Here is a worked example for an 800 W kit in a state that gets about 5 peak sun hours a day:

  • 800 W × 5 hours = 4,000 Wh, or 4 kWh on a perfect day.
  • 4 kWh × 365 days = 1,460 kWh a year.
  • Take off about 20% for shade, panel angle, heat and inverter losses: 1,460 × 0.8 ≈ 1,170 kWh a year.

The average US home uses about 10,500 kWh a year, according to the EIA. So this kit covers roughly 11% of a typical home's use. At $0.17 per kWh, 1,170 kWh is worth about $199 a year.

Balcony panels are often mounted upright on a railing. A vertical panel can make 20% to 40% less than one tilted toward the sun, so plan for that if your setup is flat against a wall.

What does it actually power?

A plug-in kit offsets your base load. That is the power your home draws all the time, often 100 to 300 W. If your kit makes 600 W at noon and your home is using 250 W, most homes send the extra 350 W back to the grid. Whether you get paid for it depends on your utility, and many plug-in rules do not offer credit. That is why smaller kits that match your base load often pay off best. Size your kit to your base load, not your whole bill. Check your smart meter data or utility app for your lowest overnight use. If it sits near 200 W, a kit of 400 to 800 W is usually the sweet spot.

Is plug-in solar safe?

It can be, if you follow a few rules. Most of the risk comes from cheap gear and overloaded circuits.

  • Buy certified equipment. In the US, look for UL-listed microinverters and kits tested to plug-in standards such as UL 3700. Skip uncertified kits sold with "suicide cords," which have bare male plugs on both ends.
  • Do not exceed the circuit rating. A standard 15 A, 120 V circuit is rated for 1,800 W, and continuous loads should stay at 80% or less. Solar power and your appliance loads share that wire.
  • Think about a dedicated circuit. If your outlet shares a circuit with a space heater or microwave, the wire could carry more current than the breaker sees. A circuit with nothing else heavy on it is safer.
  • Plug straight into the wall. No extension cords, power strips or adapters.
  • Mount panels securely. A balcony panel can catch wind like a sail. Use rated brackets and check your building's rules.

If you want a new outlet, a dedicated circuit or any change to your electrical panel, hire a licensed electrician. Do not open your panel yourself.

Is plug-in solar legal in the US?

As of October 2026, 10 states have enacted plug-in solar laws. Utah went first in 2025. These laws generally let you plug in a certified kit with anti-islanding protection without going through the full interconnection process used for rooftop systems.

What about renters and HOAs?

State laws cover the grid side. Your lease, condo board or HOA may still have rules about what hangs on a balcony. Ask in writing first. Our solar for renters guide covers other options if the answer is no.

How does Germany handle balcony solar?

Germany is the world's biggest balcony solar market, with more than 1 million registered units. Its rules are a useful model.

  • Power limit: 800 W of inverter output, with up to 2,000 Wp of panels connected. These limits came with the Solarpaket I reforms in May 2024.
  • Registration: You must register the system in the Marktstammdatenregister, the national energy registry, within one month. It is a short online form.
  • Tenants: Since October 2024, renters and condo owners have a legal claim to install one. A landlord can refuse only for a valid reason, such as building safety.

Why allow more panels than inverter output? Extra panels help on cloudy days and in the morning and evening, so the inverter runs near its limit for more hours.

Is plug-in solar right for you?

A plug-in kit makes sense if you have a sunny balcony, patio or yard that faces south, east or west. It also fits renters who cannot put panels on a roof, people who want a low-cost first step and anyone curious about solar. It is not the right tool if you want to cover most of your bill or keep power on during outages. For that, a rooftop system and a battery work better. Frequently asked questions

Will a plug-in solar kit work during a power outage?

No. Anti-islanding shuts the microinverter off when the grid goes down. You need a battery or power station with its own outlets for backup.

Can I plug a solar kit into any outlet?

Use a standard grounded outlet on a circuit without other heavy loads, and never use an extension cord. Have a licensed electrician check the circuit if you are unsure.

Will my meter spin backward?

It depends on your meter and utility. Some meters credit exported power, some ignore it and some flag it, so check with your utility first.

How big a plug-in kit can I use in the US?

As of October 2026, most of the 10 states with plug-in laws allow up to 1,200 W, and Colorado allows 1,920 W per meter. Other states may require interconnection approval.

Do I need to register a balcony solar system in Germany?

Yes. You must register it in the Marktstammdatenregister within one month of starting it up.
