Solar panel maintenance and cleaning: what actually matters

Solar panels need little care, but a few checks matter. Learn when cleaning pays off, how to do it safely and what to inspect each year.

By the Simple.solar editorial team. Reviewed October 2026. 6-minute read. How we research.

Short answerSolar panels need very little upkeep. Check your monitoring app monthly, clean only when dirt clearly cuts output, and plan for an inverter replacement around years 10 to 15.

Solar panels have no moving parts. Rain washes most dust away. Many systems run for years with no hands-on work at all. That is part of why solar is such a low-stress upgrade.

Low upkeep is not zero upkeep, though. A failed inverter, a pigeon nest or a thick layer of pollen can quietly cost you money for months. This guide covers the few things that actually matter and skips the ones that do not.

Why is your monitoring app the most important tool?

Almost every modern system comes with an app or web portal. It shows how much power your panels make each day. Checking it is the single best maintenance habit you can build, and it takes about a minute.

You are not looking for exact numbers. You are looking for changes. Good habits include:

  • Glance at daily output once a week or so.
  • Compare this month to the same month last year, not to last month. Seasons change output a lot.
  • Watch for a day of zero output on a sunny day. That often points to a tripped breaker or an inverter fault.
  • If you have microinverters or optimizers, look for one panel that makes much less than the others.

Here is a worked example. Your system made 820 kWh last July. This July it made 700 kWh, and the weather was similar. That is a drop of 120 kWh, or 120 ÷ 820 = about 15%. A drop that big is worth a closer look. A drop of 2% to 3% could just be weather.

Panels lose a little output each year as they age, usually about 0.5% per year. After 10 years, a panel making 95% of its starting output is normal. A sudden drop is not.

How much output does dirt really cost?

Dirt on panels is called soiling. How much it hurts depends on where you live. Studies from NREL and other labs show a wide range.

ClimateTypical yearly soiling lossMain cause
Rainy (Pacific Northwest, Southeast)1% to 2%Pollen, leaves
Mixed (Midwest, Northeast)2% to 4%Pollen, bird droppings
Dry and dusty (Southwest deserts)5% to 10% or moreDust, long dry spells
Near farms, roads or constructionCan be higherField dust, soot

Tilt matters too. Steep panels shed dirt better when it rains. Flat or low-tilt panels hold dust and dry puddle marks along the bottom edge.

When is cleaning your panels worth it?

Do the math before you pay for cleaning. Say you have a 7 kW system that makes 10,000 kWh a year. You pay 16 cents per kWh. That output is worth 10,000 × $0.16 = $1,600 a year.

  • In a rainy area with a 2% loss, dirt costs you about $32 a year.
  • In a desert with an 8% loss, dirt costs you about $128 a year.

A professional cleaning often runs $100 to $300. In the rainy example, paying for cleaning loses money. In the desert, one cleaning before the sunny season might break even or come out a bit ahead.

Clean when you can see a clear problem: bird droppings covering part of a panel, a heavy coat of pollen after a dry spring, or a layer of dust after months with no rain. Spot dirt matters more than you might think, because a covered cell can drag down a whole string of panels on some systems.

How do you clean solar panels safely?

Safety comes first. Falls from roofs cause serious injuries every year, and no amount of extra power is worth that. Stay on the ground whenever you can.

Do

  • Clean early in the morning or in the evening, when panels are cool. Cold water on hot glass can stress it.
  • Use a garden hose from the ground with normal pressure.
  • Use a soft brush or squeegee on a long pole for stuck dirt.
  • Use plain water. If your water is hard, use filtered or deionized water so you do not leave mineral spots.
  • Check your panel maker's warranty guide for approved cleaning methods.

Do not

  • Do not use a pressure washer. High pressure can damage seals and the coating on the glass.
  • Do not use harsh soaps, abrasive pads or solvents.
  • Do not walk on panels. It can crack cells you cannot see.
  • Do not climb onto a steep or high roof yourself. Hire a pro with fall protection.

When will you need to replace your inverter?

Panels often carry warranties of 25 years or more. Inverters usually do not last that long. The inverter turns the panels' DC power into the AC power your home uses, and it works hard every sunny day.

  • String inverters (one box, often in a garage or on a wall) usually last 10 to 15 years. Replacement often costs $1,500 to $3,000 installed.
  • Microinverters (one small unit under each panel) often carry 20 to 25 year warranties. When one fails, you replace just that unit.
  • Optimizers sit under each panel and pair with a central inverter. The optimizers last long, but the central unit may still need replacing.

Budget for this. If a string inverter costs $2,000 to replace at year 12, setting aside about $170 a year covers it. Learn more about these parts in our solar glossary. Any inverter replacement or wiring work should be done by a licensed electrician or installer.

Do you need critter guards?

The gap under rooftop panels is a dry, sheltered spot. Pigeons, squirrels and rats love it. They can build nests, leave droppings and chew wires. Chewed wires can stop your system or create a fire risk.

Critter guards are wire mesh screens clipped around the edge of the array. They do not touch the panels or void most warranties when installed correctly. Installed costs often fall between $500 and $1,500 for a typical home, depending on roof size and access.

Signs you might need them: birds flying under the panels, nesting material poking out, droppings on the roof below, or scratching sounds. It is cheaper to add guards early than to pay for cleanup and wire repair later.

Frequently asked questions

How often should solar panels be cleaned?

In most rainy climates, rarely or never. In dry, dusty areas, once or twice a year may help. Clean when your app shows a clear drop or you can see heavy dirt.

Can I use a pressure washer on solar panels?

No. High pressure can damage seals and the glass coating, and it may void your warranty. Use a garden hose and a soft brush instead.

Does rain clean solar panels?

Rain removes most loose dust, especially on tilted panels. It does not always remove bird droppings, sticky pollen or the dried edge of dust that builds up at the bottom of flat panels.

Is a maintenance plan from my installer worth it?

It depends on the price and what it covers. Many owners do fine with app checks and occasional inspections. A plan can make sense if your roof is hard to reach or you live in a dusty area.
