Solar glossary

57 solar terms, each explained in a sentence or two.

ABCDEGHIKLMNOPRSTW
AC (alternating current)
The kind of electricity your home outlets supply. Inverters convert the DC power panels make into AC.
Anti-islanding
A safety feature that shuts off a grid-tied inverter during a power outage so it can't send power into lines that utility crews are repairing.
Azimuth
The compass direction your panels face. In the Northern Hemisphere, 180° (due south) produces the most energy.
Backfeed
Power flowing from your solar system into your home's wiring or the grid. Plug-in kits backfeed through an outlet.
Balcony solar
A small plug-in solar kit, usually 300 to 1,200 W, mounted on a balcony railing, wall or yard and plugged into an outlet. Also called plug-in solar.
Battery (home)
A rechargeable unit, typically 10 to 15 kWh, that stores solar energy for evening use or outage backup.
Bifacial panel
A panel that collects light on both sides, gaining extra output from light reflected off the ground or roof.
Community solar
A shared solar farm that you subscribe to for bill credits, with no equipment at your home.
Critical loads panel
A sub-panel holding the circuits a battery keeps powered in an outage, such as the fridge, lights and Wi-Fi.
DC (direct current)
The kind of electricity solar panels and batteries produce and store.
Dealer fee
A hidden charge built into many low-APR solar loans, often 15 to 30% of the loan, that raises the system price.
Degradation
The slow loss of panel output over time, typically 0.25 to 0.5% a year.
Depth of discharge (DoD)
How much of a battery's capacity you can use. Lithium iron phosphate batteries typically allow 90 to 100%.
Derate factor
A combined allowance for real-world losses from heat, wiring, dirt and inverters. 0.75 to 0.85 is typical.
Escalator
A yearly price increase written into a solar lease or PPA, commonly 0 to 3.9% a year.
Grid-tied system
A solar system connected to the utility grid, with no battery required. It shuts off in outages.
Hybrid inverter
An inverter that manages both solar panels and a battery.
Interconnection
Your utility's approval process to connect a solar system to the grid.
Inverter
The device that converts DC power from panels into AC power for your home.
Islanding
Running your home on solar and battery while disconnected from the grid during an outage. Requires special equipment.
Kilowatt (kW)
A measure of power, the rate of energy flow. System sizes are given in kW.
Kilowatt-hour (kWh)
A measure of energy, what your utility bills you for. One kW running for one hour equals one kWh.
Lease
An arrangement where a company owns the panels on your roof and you pay a fixed monthly fee to use them.
LFP (lithium iron phosphate)
A battery chemistry known for long life and safety, used in most home batteries and power stations.
Microinverter
A small inverter attached to each panel, so shade on one panel doesn't drag down the others.
Monocrystalline
Panels made from single-crystal silicon. The most common and efficient residential type today.
MPPT charge controller
A device that gets the most power out of panels when charging a battery. Standard in RV and off-grid systems.
NABCEP
The North American Board of Certified Energy Practitioners, the leading solar installer certification.
Net billing
A program that credits exported solar power at less than the retail price, as in California's NEM 3.0.
Net metering
A program that credits solar power you export to the grid, often at the full retail price, against power you buy later.
Off-grid
A system with no utility connection, relying on panels, batteries and often a generator.
Payback period
How many years it takes for savings to equal what you paid for the system.
Peak sun hours
The number of hours per day of full-strength sunlight (1,000 W per square meter). Most US locations get 4 to 6.5.
Permission to operate (PTO)
Your utility's final sign-off allowing you to switch on a grid-tied system.
Plug-in solar
See balcony solar. Ten US states had plug-in solar laws as of October 2026.
Portable power station
A box combining a battery, inverter and outlets that you can carry and recharge with folding solar panels.
Power optimizer
A panel-level device that boosts output under shade while using a single central inverter.
PPA (power purchase agreement)
An arrangement where a company owns your system and you buy the power it makes at a set price per kWh.
Price per watt
System cost divided by its size in watts. The best way to compare quotes. Around $2.60 to $3.40 in the US in 2026.
Production guarantee
A promise that a system will make a minimum amount of energy, with payment if it falls short.
Rapid shutdown
An electrical code requirement that de-energizes rooftop wiring quickly for firefighter safety.
Round-trip efficiency
The share of energy you get back from a battery after storing it, usually 85 to 95%.
Self-consumption
Using your solar power at home as it's made, rather than exporting it.
Smart Export Guarantee (SEG)
The UK scheme that requires certain suppliers to pay for exported solar power.
Soft costs
Non-hardware costs such as sales, permits, labor and overhead. Often half or more of a US solar quote.
Solar Renewable Energy Certificate (SREC)
A tradable certificate earned for each 1,000 kWh of solar power in some US states.
STC (small-scale technology certificate)
An Australian certificate that discounts the upfront cost of solar panels and batteries.
String inverter
A single central inverter connected to a string of panels. Usually the lowest-cost option.
Surge watts
The short burst of extra power a motor needs to start, often 2 to 3 times its running watts.
System size
The total rated power of your panels in kW. US homes typically install 6 to 12 kW.
Tilt
The angle of panels from horizontal. An angle near your latitude maximizes yearly output.
Time-of-use (TOU) rates
Electricity prices that change by time of day. Batteries can save money by shifting solar power to expensive evening hours.
True-up
The yearly settlement under net metering, when your credits and charges are reconciled.
Watt (W)
A unit of power. 1,000 watts equal 1 kilowatt.
Watt-hour (Wh)
A unit of energy, used for portable power stations. 1,000 Wh equal 1 kWh.
25D credit
The US Residential Clean Energy Credit, a 30% tax credit for homeowner solar. It ended for systems installed after December 31, 2025.
48E credit
A US business tax credit for clean electricity that companies that lease solar to homeowners can still claim under 2026 rules.
